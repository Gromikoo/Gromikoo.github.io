01

The call

A recruiting-agency client needed to know which employers would actually work with an outside recruiter. That can't be scraped, so the agent asks. This is its first live call that reached a human.

Live outbound · RetellNames redacted
AgentHits an auto-attendant reading out ten names and extensions. Identifies the target, dials the extension. No human involved.
HR mgr"Human resources, at your service."
Agent"Are you filling that one in-house, or would you look at candidates from an agency you haven't worked with before?"
HR mgr"Well, it's already been filled. I closed it out Friday afternoon."
Agent"Got it. Thanks for letting me know." Ends without pushing.
The job board still had that role listed as open. The agent surfaced something no scraper could reach — a fact that existed only in someone's head.

The extraction rule No verbatim quote, no verdict. The grader refuses to return an answer when nothing was captured, because a claim about an employer with nothing quoted is an inference, not data. Recording available on request.

02

What broke, and what I changed

Four live calls produced four failures. None of them showed up in testing — they only exist once a real person is on the line. This is the loop I would bring to a customer's deployment.

Receptionist said the contact no longer worked there. The agent thanked her and hung up — on the one person who knew who had replaced her. Added a "who's handling that now?" branch that takes the new name and asks to be transferred. Reflex sympathy banned in the prompt.
Told the name was wrong, the agent repeated the same name. The firm asked to be removed from the list — permanently. Separate branch for "no such person here." Removal now enforced in code at three points, matched on phone number and company name.
Offered voicemail, the agent froze and repeated "hello" four times. Explicit voicemail path, then eight browser tests that must all pass before any batch dials.
Agent narrated its own reasoning aloud on the line, then sat through the whole phone tree twice — 45 wasted seconds per call. Prompt now forbids speaking its reasoning, and dials the extension on first hearing instead of waiting out the menu.
03

Guardrails and enablement

Every failure above became a written rule or a gate. Nothing important is left as a note — prose is a guideline, a gate is a gate.

Pre-dial gateBlocks a batch on a do-not-call match, a malformed number the platform would silently drop, a duplicate firm, or a contact whose title can't make the decision. Negative-tested both ways — proven to block, not just to pass.
RunbookNine numbered steps from hearing a bad call to redialing a fixed agent, including the two steps people skip. Written so someone else can run it without me.
Test scriptEight scenarios — wrong person, no such person, voicemail, "take me off your list," "are you a robot," and a clean yes. Free, ten minutes, run before any change reaches a phone.
Row specWritten before the first call, so results couldn't be graded generously afterwards. Five rules, all five, or the record is "not checked" — never a "no."
ComplianceBusiness landlines only, no mobiles. Removal requests are permanent and enforced at three separate points in the pipeline.
04

The pipeline feeding it

An agent is only as good as the record it dials, so I built that half too.

SourceLive job-board APIs across two metros, diffed weekly for new, still-open and filled roles.
VerifyEvery phone and email checked against the company's own website. No pattern-guessed addresses, ever.
IdentityProves the named company owns the domain, email and phone on the record — after a mis-resolved listing once joined two companies into one.
OperateUnattended on a Linux VPS via cron, with a canary on the upstream feed and a second machine watching daily. A job that never runs emits no error.
Deliver195 accounts across seven weeks, zero duplicates, enforced by an append-only ledger.
